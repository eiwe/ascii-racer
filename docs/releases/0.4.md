Something to race: a red AI opponent with five difficulty levels, start lights, a sunset sky as the new default, night racing with headlights, and an experimental build that runs in the browser.

## New in 0.4

- **AI opponent.** A red speedster starts beside you on the grid. Set its difficulty in the pause menu: off, or 1 to 5. It drives the same car with the same setup as you and gets no catch-up help; higher levels simply drive closer to the limit, and levels 4 and 5 drift the hairpin and use nitro. Contact is solid in both directions. Your position and the gap in seconds show at the bottom left.
- **Start lights.** Every start from the grid runs five red lights with a beep each, a random hold, then lights out with a higher beep. The cars are held until then (you can still rev), and the lap timer and race music start at lights out.
- **Sunset is the default look.** Lighting `1` is now a low sun under a sunset sky with drifting cumulus clouds. The sky shows in the chase view and the demo reel's cinematic shots.
- **Night racing** (lighting `2`, experimental). Moonlight, headlight beams that light the road ahead and throw moving shadows, tail lamps that brighten under braking, and a sky with a moon and stars. Both cars have lights.
- **Zoom that fits the window.** In the isometric view, a 16:9 or narrower window now looks further ahead of the car and zooms out a little, so it shows about as much road ahead as an ultrawide. The pause menu has a ZOOM setting: AUTO (the default) or fixed steps from 0.85 to 1.50.
- **Web build** (experimental). The game runs in a desktop browser with WebGL 2. See "Web build" below.
- **Faster, more planted car.** The default handling has more road grip, a rear that holds on, and a top speed of about 11.6 (was 9.8). The tight corners still need braking; the faster bends are now flat. The `drift` preset is the one that steps out. The jump was reshaped for the higher speed.
- **Quieter tyres, louder race music.** The tyres squeal only near and past the grip limit, not through every corner.
- **Debug HUD starts minimised.** Press `H` to open it; a small note in the top left corner says so.

The desktop builds need a GPU with Vulkan (Windows, Linux), Direct3D 12 (Windows) or Metal (macOS).

## Web build

The web build is the same game on a different renderer (Godot's Compatibility renderer, WebGL 2). It needs a current desktop Chrome or Firefox.

- Opening `index.html` straight from disk does not work. Serve the unzipped folder from any static web server, for example `python3 -m http.server` run inside it, then open the address it prints.
- The browser stays silent until the first key press, so the demo reel has no music until you start.
- The picture is close to the desktop builds but not identical: the browser renderer lights scenes differently. The isometric view and the classic mode match closely; night and the far distance in the chase view differ most.
- The sound has not been listened to in a browser yet, and gamepads are untested there.

## First launch on macOS

The macOS build is not signed with an Apple Developer ID, so macOS blocks it the first time. Either:

- Open the app, dismiss the warning, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click **Open Anyway**; or
- after unzipping, run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Downloads/"ASCII Racer.app"` in Terminal (adjust the path).

## Known limitations

- The opponent is a rival on the road, not yet a race: there is a start, but no lap count, finish or results. Only its engine is heard, and it is not panned left or right.
- Night is experimental. The far side of a corner outside the headlight beam is sparse, and in the chase view an opponent's headlights from behind wash out your car.
- In AUTO zoom on a 16:9 window the car sits behind the centre of the screen at speed and is drawn a little smaller. A fixed zoom step in the pause menu keeps it centred.
- The start beeps and the opponent's engine level were set by judgement, not measured.
- A `custom` handling preset saved in an earlier version keeps its old values.
- The `heavy` preset bottoms out on the jump landing.
- The Retina defaults on macOS are untested, and the Windows build has only been run under Wine, not on a Windows PC.
- One track, and one opponent at a time.
