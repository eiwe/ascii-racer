The first Android build of ASCII Racer: a phone version steered by tilting the phone. This is a preview for testing. **It has not yet been run on a real phone**; everything was checked on a desktop that simulates the phone's layout and input.

Only the Android build is attached. Windows, Linux, macOS and the browser are unchanged from [0.5](https://github.com/eiwe/ascii-racer/releases/tag/v0.5).

## Install

Download `ascii-racer-0.6-android.1-android-arm64.apk` on the phone and open it. Android asks once to allow installs from the browser or file manager you opened it with. It needs a 64-bit ARM phone (nearly every phone from the last several years) with OpenGL ES 3.

## Controls

| Action | Phone |
|---|---|
| Steer | Tilt the phone like a steering wheel: right side down steers right. Full lock is at about 30 degrees, with a small dead zone around level. |
| Throttle | Automatic once the start lights go out. |
| Brake, reverse | **BRAKE** (bottom left). Held at a standstill it reverses; tilt to steer out of a wall. |
| Nitro | **NITRO** (bottom right). |
| Pause | **\|\|** (top right) or the back button. Back again resumes. |
| Menus | Tap anywhere on the demo reel for the level select. Tap the arrows or swipe sideways to change circuit, tap a row to change it, tap START. |

Races start in the rear chase view on the phone. The pause menu has three phone-only rows:

- **CAMERA**: chase or isometric.
- **GLYPH SIZE**: the characters are sized to about 60 rows of text on the screen's height; step them larger or smaller.
- **FLIP STEERING**: turn this on if tilting right steers left on your phone.

A connected keyboard or gamepad works as on the desktop.

## What to expect

- The sensor directions were taken from Godot's source, not confirmed on a device. If steering is mirrored, use FLIP STEERING.
- The frame rate on a phone is unknown. The build uses the same lightweight renderer as the browser version.
- Unverified on a device: the notch and rounded-corner margins, the back button, pausing when the app goes to the background, and several fingers at once.
- There is no handbrake button, so handbrake drifts need a gamepad.
- The picture turns with the phone as you steer. Keeping it level with the horizon is planned as an option.
