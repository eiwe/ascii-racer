The second Android preview adds the level display: as you tilt the phone to steer, the picture turns the other way, so the game's horizon stays level with the real one. **Like the first preview, it has not yet been run on a real phone**; everything was checked on a desktop that simulates the phone's layout and input.

Only the Android build is attached. Windows, Linux, macOS and the browser are unchanged from [0.5](https://github.com/eiwe/ascii-racer/releases/tag/v0.5). This build installs over 0.6-android.1.

## New in this build

- **Level display.** During a race the world is drawn on a larger grid of characters and turned against the phone's roll, up to 30 degrees either way; past that the picture turns with the phone. The characters stay level with the world, so they do not flicker as your hands move. The buttons, the nitro bar, the start lights and the menus stay fixed to the phone. It is on by default; **LEVEL DISPLAY** in the pause menu turns it off.
- **Pause menu rows are easier to hit.** Each row's tap area now covers its full height, with no gaps between rows.

## Install

Download `ascii-racer-0.6-android.2-android-arm64.apk` on the phone and open it. Android asks once to allow installs from the browser or file manager you opened it with. It needs a 64-bit ARM phone with OpenGL ES 3.

## Controls

| Action | Phone |
|---|---|
| Steer | Tilt the phone like a steering wheel: right side down steers right. Full lock is at about 30 degrees, with a small dead zone around level. |
| Throttle | Automatic once the start lights go out. |
| Brake, reverse | **BRAKE** (bottom left). Held at a standstill it reverses; tilt to steer out of a wall. |
| Nitro | **NITRO** (bottom right). |
| Pause | **\|\|** (top right) or the back button. Back again resumes. |
| Menus | Tap anywhere on the demo reel for the level select. Tap the arrows or swipe sideways to change circuit, tap a row to change it, tap START. |

Phone-only rows in the pause menu: **CAMERA** (chase, the phone's default, or isometric), **GLYPH SIZE**, **FLIP STEERING** and **LEVEL DISPLAY**.

## What to expect

- The sensor directions were taken from Godot's source, not confirmed on a device. If steering is mirrored, or the picture turns the wrong way and doubles the tilt, turn on FLIP STEERING: it flips both.
- The level display draws about twice as many characters. If the game runs slowly, turn LEVEL DISPLAY off first.
- The level display follows your hands with a short delay (the simulation assumed 40 ms), so the horizon moves a little during a quick turn of the phone and settles when you stop.
- The frame rate on a phone is unknown.
- Unverified on a device: the notch and rounded-corner margins, the back button, pausing when the app goes to the background, and several fingers at once.
- There is no handbrake button, so handbrake drifts need a gamepad.
- With the full pause menu (14 rows) the rows are about 3 mm apart on a typical phone, which may be tight.
